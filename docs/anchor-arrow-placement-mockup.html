Arrow placement at a hub

Your 4-step case: ② is anchored to ① but fires 0:30 before it (−0:30), so ② sits to the left; and ③ & ④ are anchored to ②, firing +0:30 after (to the right). So all three of ②'s lines meet on its right — one arriving (① → ②) and two leaving (② → ③, ② → ④). Every arrow points at its anchorer.

The only tricky arrow is ① → ② — it points at ②, the hub. Right at ②'s pin it lands in the shared trunk where all three lines overlap, so you can't tell which line it belongs to. Placed just past the branch, on the segment that's ② → ①'s alone, it's unmistakable — and still as close to ② as it can get.
NAIVEArrow at the pin
The ① → ② arrowhead sits at ②, inside the cluster where the trunk and both branches meet — it could belong to any of the three lines.
PROPOSEDBacked off to the clear run
The arrow slides back along ① → ② only until it clears the branch — on that line's own run — pointing left at ②. Closest unambiguous spot to the anchorer.
Same rule for every connector: the head points at the anchorer and sits as close to it as possible without entering another line's space. On a plain (right-of-anchor) dependent that's just at its pin, as today; only a hub pulls it back. Needs negative step-offsets enabled (like the on-/off-air anchors already are) + routing that lets a dependent sit left of its anchor.
