Why the arrow can't flip on you

The worry: if you re-anchor so the left step depends on a right step, an arrow would point backward and the roles blur. But that state can't exist — because of the ordering rule, flipping the anchor reorders the steps in time, and the line still flows left → right.

⚓ The invariant: a dependent always fires at anchor_edge + offset, with offset ≥ 0 (the drag clamps to it; the agent rejects a negative offset). So the dependent is always at or after its anchor in time — the anchor is always the earlier / left end, the dependent the later / right end. The arrow always points right.
BEFOREPOWER hangs off BW
BW is the anchor, POWER the dependent — POWER = BW + 0:30. The line leaves BW and the arrow lands on POWER.
AFTER FLIPBW hangs off POWER
Re-anchor BW → POWER and BW must now fire after POWER, so POWER slides earlier (left). It's the anchor now — still on the left, arrow still pointing right, into BW.
So "which is which" is never ambiguous: the step a line leaves (its left / earlier end) is the anchor; the step the arrowhead lands on (its right / later end) is the dependent. The faint arrowhead only confirms what the left-to-right flow already tells you. You can never get a right-hand step anchoring a left-hand one — the editor would need a negative offset, which it refuses (offset ≥ 0); it moves the anchor earlier instead.
