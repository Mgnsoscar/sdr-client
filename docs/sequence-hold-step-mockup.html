Hold step, as a window

The Hold becomes a two-line window — enter on the left, resume on the right — carrying the relative hatch and no interior ticks, since its length is set live on Proceed. When a step with duration is anchored to resume at offset ≥ 0, a fresh absolute time region opens after the hold — its own ruler with 0 at resume — so that step lands at its true time. Without one, steps just snap to an edge.

Two lines, one window

The Hold is a window, not a mark: enter → resume. Its width is set live on Proceed, so it wears the relative hatch and carries no ticks inside — the task holds its state across it.

Snap to an edge

Anchor a step to the Hold and its offset picks the side: < 0 on the enter line, ≥ 0 on the resume line. With no region, everything snaps to an edge — nothing lands inside the window.

Duration opens a region

A step with a duration anchored to resume (offset ≥ 0) opens a new absolute region after the hold — 0 at resume, its own ticks — and every resume-step then sits at its true time on that ruler.

Toggle a down-ramp after resume to watch the region open and the resume steps move from the edge onto their true time. Drag offset from Hold across zero to see the new step pick a side.
