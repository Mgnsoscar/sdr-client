Hold step, as a window

Once a Hold is in the sequence, the run’s end can’t be scheduled — off-air floats to wherever Proceed lands. So the Hold takes the place of the old relative band: it becomes the one variable-length section. Everything after it is a fixed-length block that slides along with resume, shown as up to two absolute windows — a green resume window, then a red off-air window — each present only when a step is anchored in it, and the two never overlap.

The Hold is the only relative part

With a Hold, the end can’t be scheduled — off-air floats to Proceed. The Hold replaces the relative band: it’s the sole variable-length section, hatched with no interior ticks.

Resume window

Steps anchored to resume live in a green absolute window right of the Hold — 0 at resume, counting forward. It appears only when something is anchored there.

Off-air window

Steps anchored to off-air live in a red absolute window — 0 at off-air, counting back. It appears only when something is anchored there, and never overlaps the green one — the two abut into one floating block.

Toggle the two switches to add or remove an anchored step and watch each absolute window appear or fold away. The Hold’s own anchor can be on-air or another step (never off-air — that would be circular).
