What changed in this round

  • Colour = the task, as in the sequence editor (GPS C/A is teal wherever it runs). A sequence is now a neutral frame: the slate group band, and a pill or window bar whose green left edge is its on-air and red right edge its off-air. Expanded, the same green and red lines run down through all of its rows.
  • Warm-up and cool-down are drawn. The hatched stubs on a task bar (and on the pill) are the part of the task that runs outside the sequence's on-air window — the muted pre-roll launch 1–5 s before on-air and the stop after off-air. They get a minimum width so a 1 s lead-in is still visible.
  • Steps are edited here. The step tools add to the selected sequence; drag a tune or ramp to move it; double-click opens the same step / ramp editor the sequence editor uses; the right-click menus are the sequence editor's.
  • Anything anchors to anything. A sequence's on-air or off-air edge can hang off another sequence's edge, a task's edge or a step — on any unit. Here the CW drift's on-air is anchored to the reacquisition sweep's off-air (+0:30), and Galileo E1 on Broadcaster 3 is anchored to the chirp's up-ramp end on Broadcaster 2 (+1:00). Drag a handle onto another handle to make one; the connector's chip is the offset.
  • Overlap on one unit is no longer drawn on top of itself: a collapsed unit packs its pills into sub-lanes, and an overlap is flagged (red outline + header) rather than hidden. Drag the CW drift left to see it.
  • Unit banners stick to the left edge while the canvas scrolls.

Open questions for this round

  1. Is the neutral frame enough to say "this task belongs to that sequence"? The band, the row header hierarchy and the green/red edges carry it now. The alternative is a small sequence tag on every task bar, which the sequence editor deliberately dropped as clutter.
  2. Cross-unit anchors at arm time. The plan resolves the whole anchor graph into each unit's absolute on-air / off-air when it is scheduled or armed, then arms every unit with plain absolute times — the units' clocks are already NTP-synced and checked at arm. A cycle (A after B after A) is refused at save, like step cycles are today.
  3. The sequence's own off-air. With anchoring in place, a fixed-length sequence is just "off-air anchored to my own on-air + 7:30" (the reacquisition sweep here), so no separate "fixed length" switch is needed. Agree?
  4. Conflict rule. Two sequences on one unit conflict when their drawn spans (steps, warm-up and cool-down included) overlap. Should the plan refuse to save with a conflict, or only warn and let the unit refuse at arm?
  5. Holds — parked for later, as agreed.
